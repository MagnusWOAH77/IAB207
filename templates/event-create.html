Enhanced Acknowledgement guidance

A meaningful Acknowledgement of Country should be specific to the event location, recognise the relevant Traditional Custodians or Traditional Owners, and avoid treating the acknowledgement as a generic formality.

Use reliable sources to check the Traditional Custodians of the event location. A useful starting point is the AIATSIS Map of Indigenous Australia . You may also use AIATSIS Whose Country, Reconciliation Australia, Know Your Country, and local council or local Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander organisation resources.

Common mistakes to avoid
  • Do not guess Traditional Custodian names.
  • Do not use a Welcome to Country unless it is delivered by an authorised Traditional Owner.
  • Do not copy text without checking whether it fits the event location.
  • Do not overstate certainty where Traditional Owner information may be complex or contested.
  • Try to connect the acknowledgement to the event, place, and community context.

General guide: Acknowledgement of Country

An Acknowledgement of Country recognises the Traditional Custodians of the land where an event is held and pays respect to Elders past and present. It supports reconciliation by recognising the continuing connection of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples to land, waters, culture and community.

A Welcome to Country is different. It is delivered by Traditional Owners, or Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people with authority to welcome visitors to Country. Event creators should not present their own acknowledgement as a Welcome to Country.
